# sous — INT8 K/V through the attention tile (the `kv_int8` mirror)

*2026-10-12. The in-tree design for the L4 lever of the block-5 round profile (profile report of 2026-10-12, the maintainer's `sous-spikes/prof-b5/results.md`) and its stage-1 spike (`sous-spikes/l4-int8kv/results.md`, same day). Grounded on the maintainer's M5 Pro (applegpu_g17s, 20 GPU cores, macOS 27.0, mlx 0.32.3, mlx-vlm 0.7.2, the default Qwen3.8-27B-4bit with DFlash2). The maintainer ruled "take i8q to stage 2 and PR the relay first" on 2026-10-12; the split-QK relay is its own PR (`feat/attention-tile-split-qk`), this design assumes it is in main, and every ratio below is against that relay tile. Before commit, three adversarial reviews checked the first draft against the tree, mlx-vlm 0.7.2's source and the spike's data; their findings are folded in, and the departures from the spike's registered stage-2 shape are listed at the end.*

## What the measurements established

**The attention tile is bandwidth-bound at T = 1 and 2 and issue-bound from the second 16-row fragment group (T = 3) on.** With the relay, its time at 57K keys per 16-layer chain is 13.2–13.3 ms at T = 1 and 13.8 ms at T = 5, reading K/V at 270–283 GB/s; before the relay a window diagnostic (every address pinned to one 32-key block) still cost 0.93 of the streaming time at T = 5, and the relay changed T ≤ 5 by nothing.

**Halving the K/V bytes alone does not pay at T = 5.** The spike's first variant (int8 K/V, the tile's load pattern) was 1.8× at T = 1 and 0.83× at T = 3..5. Three further changes, each measured on the ktime chain with an A/A pair, carried it over the bar (ms per chain at 57K; `bf16` is the relay tile, `bf16s` in the spike, 13.79 ms at T = 5):

| variant | T = 5 | ratio | T = 1 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| relay tile (in-tree after the relay PR) | 13.79 | 1.00 | 13.26 | 1.00 |
| int8 K/V, 16-byte V and scale loads (26 load instructions per lane-block, not 48) | 11.58 | 1.19 | 7.23 | 1.83 |
| + split-QK relay | 10.31 | 1.34 | 7.03 | 1.89 |
| + int8 Q, QK on the `int8_t × int8_t → int32_t` rows (`i8q`) | **8.17** | **1.69** | 7.33 | 1.81 |

`i8q` is 1.65× at 44K and 1.72× at 80K at T = 5; at T = 6 and 8 it is 1.16× the relay tile (the relay already removed most of the third fragment group's cost; the int8 kernel brings part of it back). It comes from one timing job (22 paired rounds per cell); the replicate with a new buffer seed and the variant order reversed covered the variants without int8 Q and reproduced their ratios within 0.015. On the real 35K-token tools fork `i8q` is 1.60× at T = 5. Converting int8 to bf16 in registers instead of feeding MPP's mixed rows is 0.80× at T = 5. Two things this design changes were not timed in the spike and are decided by the gate's kernel step: the spike quantised Q on the host (about nine small mlx ops per layer), this design does it in the kernel; the spike read separate K and V buffers, this design reads one packed buffer.

**Quantisation error is small on real K/V.** After `k_norm` the keys have no outlier channels (the largest channel's amax is 2.5–4.9× the median channel's), so one symmetric scale per (token, KV head) costs 1.0 % relative RMS on K and V. On the fork's 16 full-attention layers, the fp32 attention output with int8 K/V against bf16 K/V is 0.73 % median / 1.76 % max with channel-matched Gaussian queries and 0.86 % / 1.59 % with peaked queries (`kvstats`); the error rises from 0.6 % in the early layers to 1.0–1.8 % in layers 19–31. Group-64 scales cost 0.46–0.67 % median by the same measure; they do not fold into the tile's permutation and stay the fallback if the suite objects. The spike's kernel-level quality check ran on one layer (layer 3, the least sensitive): 0.15 % with Gaussian queries and 0.71 % with peaked queries against the bf16 tile, with int8 Q adding 0.04 %. The gate's quality step runs the final kernel over all 16 layers.

**The kernel is exact in the tile's sense.** Every verify row equals its one-row call bit for bit (235/235 rows at N0, the next two step boundaries, 35,001 real tokens and 57,601, T = 1..8), the NaN fence past n holds, and the output is within 2.0e-3 relative RMS of an fp32 reference on the same codes (K, V and Q).

**Per-round quantisation is dispatch-bound.** Quantising a round's five appended rows for 16 layers costs 0.46 ms in absolute terms through a fused kernel plus two slice updates per layer (48 dispatches; 1.65 ms through plain mlx ops), against the stage-1 bar of 0.30 ms that the maintainer ruled past, and against a 5.6 ms saving per block-5 round at 57K. The one-time build of the mirror for a 57K prefix is bandwidth-bound: 57,000 rows × 4 heads × (1,024 B read + 516 B written) × 16 layers = 5.6 GB, 20 ms at 280 GB/s as a single pass; it scales to 46 ms at 131K and 92 ms at 262K.

## Decisions

1. **An int8 mirror beside the bf16 cache, built and read only inside `decode()`.** The KVCache stays bf16 and is what every `prefill()` call, the forks, the fork store, the slots and every existing gate see. Inside `VLMEngine.decode()` the kv8 path serves one-row decode and verify attention at n ≥ N0 from an int8 copy of the committed rows that lives on the cache object for that call only. That includes the one-row forward that ends the decode call's own prefill segment (the seven-token generation prompt on the hybrid path, the whole prompt on the cold paths): its KV never outlives the turn, because the hybrid path restores the anchor snapshot after the decode and the cold paths discard the cache (qwen3_5 caches are never all trimmable, so the trimmable branch that would keep one is unreachable). Nothing kv8 serves reaches a stored fork or a slot.
2. **Per-token symmetric int8 with fp16 scales for K and V; int8 Q with an fp32 scale per row, quantised inside the kernel.** Scale = amax / 127, round to nearest even, codes in [−127, 127], a zero row gives scale 0 and codes 0. The K scale multiplies the finished score, the V scale folds into the probability, the Q scale multiplies the score: no dequantisation in the loop.
3. **The verify rows read the same codes as decode rows.** A (row, key) score depends on the row's codes and scale, the key's codes and scale, and nothing else; the mirror is a pure per-row function of the bf16 rows, so bulk and incremental quantisation agree bit for bit, and a verify row equals the one-row call at its own length. Greedy output with the drafter equals greedy output without it, as for the tile.
4. **The mirror follows the cache's offset, not the calls kv8 happens to serve.** Rows are re-quantised whenever the cache has rewritten them: the mirror's valid length shrinks with every `trim` on the cache (an instance-level wrap installed with the mirror), every hooked attention call on a mirrored cache clamps it to the committed prefix (`n − T`), and a mirror found with another decode's serial is rebuilt from row 0. This closes the hole the first draft had: a call that appends rows without being served (below N0, declined, out of scope, or an "off" round of the gate's shim) can no longer leave stale codes under a later served call.
5. **One flag, `[model].kv_int8`, default `true`, refused where the tile is not active.** The kernel rides on the tile (its platform gate, S, N0, the chunk schedule, `verify_plan`, `MAX_RUN`, the reduce kernel and, with a drafter, exact verify attention), so it inherits every tile refusal. A typo warns and means `false`.
6. **Memory: admitted per decode against live headroom; the reserve, the slot budget and the pressure valve do not change.** The mirror costs 0.504 × the context's bf16 KV (516 B per token per head against 1,024) for the length of one decode. Scaling the window-sized reserve by that would cut the automatic slot budget by 4–8 GiB (25–45 % on the M5 Pro at the 131072 and 262144 windows) and buy cache misses that cost more than the lever saves. Instead, the first served call of a decode admits the mirror only if Metal's live headroom covers the mirror's bytes for the cache's capacity plus a 1 GiB margin; otherwise that decode runs on the bf16 tile and a counter says so. The mirror's bytes count toward the valve's headroom exactly as the turn's own KV growth does, and the gate measures evictions and slot hits on both arms.
7. **The fork key and epoch do not change for this feature.** Nothing kv8 serves is persisted (decision 1), so the key gains no field, `kv8.py` stays out of `_EPOCH_FILES`, and the two new kernel sources are excluded from the epoch by name with that reason, as `verifyattn.py` is: the glob would otherwise cost every user one cold fork store (a cold first turn was 175 s against 15.7 s from disk) for no numeric reason.
8. **`sous tune` keys its suite rows by the flag** (`Arm.kv_int8`, `SuiteRun.kv_int8`), as it does for the tile and the projection kernel, because the flag changes numerics; the gate's suite step needs the on and off arms kept apart in one run directory.

## Design

### Module layout

- `src/sous/engine/kv8.py` — the mirror and its wraps, the quantiser dispatch, the kernel dispatch, `availability()`, `enable()`, `clear()`, the probe, `calls`, the source pins.
- `src/sous/engine/kernels/attention_tile_kv8.metal` — the split body over int8 K/V/Q (the relay kernel with int8 operands); the tile's reduce kernel is reused unchanged.
- `src/sous/engine/kernels/kv8_quant.metal` — the fused row quantiser (MPP-free: it compiles on any Metal GPU and CI tests it).
- Seams in existing files: `tileattn._serve_decode` and `tileattn.serve_verify` (which gains the `cache` argument `verifyattn._wrap` already holds) hand a served call to `kv8`; `VLMEngine` enables it after the projection kernel, sets the serving flag around `stream_generate`, drops the mirrors in `decode()`'s `finally`, clears it in `unload()`; `LMEngine` carries an `off` status; `ManagedEngine` passes the status through; `base.default_engine_factory` passes the config; `config.py` parses the key; the load line and the status document carry its state; `sous tune` keys rows by it; `forkstore.engine_epoch` excludes the two kernel files by name.

### Module state and the serving window

Two plain ints, never a model reference:

- `_active` — 1 only after `enable()` passed every gate and the probe; the first statement of every `enable()` clears it; `clear()` clears it.
- `_serving` — 1 while a `decode()` runs with kv8 active. `VLMEngine.decode()` sets it immediately before `stream_generate` (inside the draft-context `seed` block, so the whole generation, its own prefill segment included, is covered) and in `finally` clears it and drops every full-attention layer's mirror (`drop_mirrors(cache)`). A module global suffices because `ManagedEngine._gen_lock` serialises every forward and both decode call sites run on the caller's session thread, cold paths included; an exception out of `stream_generate` clears it the same way.
- `serving_now()` is `_active and _serving`. Every `prefill()` call, the load-time probes and anything outside `decode()` run with it clear, so they never build or read a mirror.
- `_serial` — incremented each time `decode()` sets `_serving`; a mirror records the serial it was built under.

### The mirror

A `Mirror` object attached to a full-attention layer's `KVCache` as `_sous_kv8` (a plain attribute set with `object.__setattr__`, like the left-padding attributes mlx-vlm itself sets; `fork_copy` copies `state` and `meta_state` into a fresh cache, so a mirror never travels with a fork):

- `codes`: uint8 `[1, 4, cap, 512]`, each token's 256 K codes then its 256 V codes (the kernel reads K and V as strided views of this one buffer: token stride 512 B, V at +256; every 16-byte load stays aligned because 512 and 256 are multiples of 16);
- `sc`: fp16 `[1, 4, cap, 2]` = {sK, sV} per (token, head), contiguous in its last two axes;
- `cap`: the mirror's own capacity, a multiple of 256, at least the cache's offset at build time, grown by concatenation when a served call needs more rows. It is deliberately not `keys.shape[2]`: `KVCache.update_and_fetch` truncates to the offset before it grows, so a cache's capacity is routinely not a multiple of 4 after the first irregular prefill segment, and the kernel's 16-byte scale loads need the per-head plane stride (`cap × 4 B`) to be a multiple of 16;
- `n`: the number of valid rows, from 0;
- `serial`: the decode serial it was built under.

**Validity rule.** Three things move `n` down and one moves it up:

1. `trim`: when the mirror is installed, the cache instance's `trim` is wrapped (an instance attribute, removed with the mirror) so that after every trim `n = min(n, cache.offset)`. Inside a decode this is how DFlash rolls back rejected rows (`KVCache.trim` through `cache_state`'s transaction and rollback helpers) and the only way the offset falls.
2. Every hooked attention call on a mirrored cache, served or not, with `n_keys` keys of which the last `T` were appended by that call, runs `n = min(n, n_keys − T)` before anything else: rows below `n_keys − T` are the committed prefix, and this clamp holds even if a trim were missed.
3. A mirror whose `serial` is not the current decode's is rebuilt from row 0 (and the `finally` drop makes this a defence, not the mechanism).
4. A served call at `n_keys ≥ N0` then quantises rows `[n, n_keys)` (growing `cap` first if needed) and sets `n = n_keys`.

Consequences: the first served call of a decode quantises the whole prefix in one dispatch per layer that writes a cap-sized output used directly as the mirror (no zero-fill, no slice update; rows at or past `n` are never read because every kernel read is clamped to `n_keys − 1`); later served calls quantise T rows plus whatever a rollback replaced, two slice updates per layer; a served call below N0 (stock paths) only clamps.

**Admission** (decision 6): at the first served call of a decode, `kv8` computes the mirror's bytes for the cache's capacity across the 16 layers (`16 × 4 × cap × 516`) and admits it only if `base.metal_headroom` for this process is at least that plus 1 GiB; otherwise it marks the decode as declined (`calls["declined_memory"]`), and every hooked call of that decode takes the bf16 tile. The quantiser reads the cache's strided bf16 views in place (`ensure_row_contiguous=False`) and writes only the mirror, so the build's transient is the mirror itself.

**Lifetime.** Built on the first served call inside `decode()`, dropped (attribute and trim wrap removed) in that call's `finally`; `drop_mirrors` is also called by the probe's `finally`.

### The quantiser (`kv8_quant.metal`)

Grid (256, R, 4), threadgroup (256, 1, 1): thread d of (head, row) loads `k[row, d]` and `v[row, d]` as bf16 through the inputs' strides, takes the row's amax by `simd_max` and an 8-value threadgroup reduce, sets `s = half(amax / 127)` (a zero row gives s = 0 and codes 0; −128 is never written), writes `codes[row, d]` and `codes[row, 256 + d]` as `clamp(rint(x / float(s)), −127, 127)` and, from thread 0, `sc[row] = {sK, sV}`. Compiled with `common.h` only, `math_mode: safe`, `ensure_row_contiguous=False`. Round-to-nearest-even is the kernel's rule; the tests' reference quantiser reproduces it in mlx ops (`mx.round` rounds half away from zero in the installed mlx, so the reference corrects ties: a tie is `|x / s| − floor(|x / s|) == 0.5`, and it rounds to the even neighbour), and the tests build rows with exact ties (amax chosen so that s is exact in fp16 and some `x / s` land on .5).

### The kernel (`attention_tile_kv8.metal`)

The relay kernel with these changes, everything else as the tile (grid, threadgroup, the 32·S chunk schedule, the online softmax, the fixed-order relay, resident accumulators, the clamp past n, the zero-probability fence, the partial/stats layout):

- **Inputs** `q` bf16 `[1, 24, T, 256]`, `codes` uint8 and `sc` fp16 as above (read through strides, `ensure_row_contiguous=False`), `params`.
- **Q quantised in the kernel, staged as codes.** Each lane loads its two rows' 8 k-steps of Q (16 `bfloat4`, 64 values) into registers, takes its amax per row, reduces over the four lanes sharing a row (`simd_shuffle_xor` 1 and 8), exchanges the two halves' row maxima through threadgroup memory (one barrier), sets `sq = amax / 127` in fp32 per row (a zero row gives `sq = 0` and codes 0), and writes its values as packed int8 codes (`clamp(rint(q / sq), −127, 127)`, one `uint` per lane per k-step) into the staging array, which is therefore 4 KB per row group as the spike's was, not 8 KB; one more barrier before the loop. Three barriers per call, none per block.
- **QK** on `get_left_input_cooperative_tensor<int8_t, int8_t, int32_t>` / right the same, `multiply_accumulate` into an int32 destination zeroed per block; the relay sums int32 partials (exact: |Si| ≤ 256 × 127² < 2²⁴); then `S = float(Si) × (sq[row] × sK[key] × SCALE_LOG2)`.
- **Loads per lane per 32-key block:** K as 16-byte runs (two per key row per half), V as 16-byte runs covering two output tiles, scales as one 16-byte load per 16-key half when the whole block lies below n and clamped `half2` loads otherwise. Every K, V and scale read is clamped to `n − 1`: that clamp, not the mask, is what keeps a NaN or garbage row past n out (0 × NaN is NaN; the mask only zeroes probabilities of keys whose scale the clamp kept finite).
- **PV** on `<float, int8_t, float>` rows: `ct_p = p × sV[key]`, the row sum on the unscaled p.
- **MSL** as the tile: `remove_addrspace_t` on every cooperative-tensor `decltype`, `math_mode: safe`, `common.h + nax.h` as the header, its own kernel name. `availability()` is the shared platform rule and then a compile-and-run of every T = 1..8 and of the quantiser, success remembered only, so kv8's `nax` tests skip on kv8's own availability.

### Dispatch (`kv8.py`)

- `decode(queries, cache, splits)` and `verify(queries, keys, values, cache, splits, runs)` mirror `tileattn.decode/verify`: runs below N0 take the stock paths over the bf16 K/V passed in (unchanged, exact against each other); runs from N0 take the kv8 kernel over the mirror, cut by `verify_plan` exactly as the tile, with the tile's reduce kernel.
- `tileattn._serve_decode`: after its existing scope checks and before the N0 test, `kv8.observe(cache, n_keys=keys.shape[2], t=1)` (the clamp of validity rule 2; a no-op without a mirror); then, where the tile would run (`keys.shape[2] ≥ N0`), `if kv8.serving_now(): return kv8.decode(queries, cache, splits)`. The tile's `decode_tile` counter moves only for calls the tile itself runs; a kv8-served call moves `kv8.calls["decode_kv8"]` instead.
- `tileattn.serve_verify(queries, keys, values, splits, cache)` (the new last argument; `verifyattn._wrap` passes `cache` at its call site): `kv8.observe(cache, keys.shape[2], t)` first, then `verify_plan` and the counting as today; if `kv8.serving_now()`, the tile runs go to `kv8.verify(...)` and the tile's `verify_rows_tile`/`verify_launches` do not move (`verify_calls` and `verify_t*` still do, since they count the call); else the tile as today.
- Counters `kv8.calls`: `decode_kv8`, `verify_kv8` (calls), `verify_rows_kv8`, `verify_launches_kv8`, `mirror_builds` (first builds), `mirror_rows` (rows quantised, builds included), `mirror_trims` (trim wraps that lowered `n`), `declined_memory` (decodes admitted to the bf16 tile for headroom). Nothing is logged per turn; the per-turn gauge that stage 1's gate reads, `mirror_build_ms`, goes through the prompt cache's per-turn gauges like the drafter's counts.
- `enable(model, *, enabled, tile_status)`: first statement clears `_active` (and `_serving`); `off` → `{"state": "off", "reason": None, "probe_seconds": None}`; `unavailable` with one INFO line (`expected=True`) when the tile is not `active` (its reason or `attention tile off`); then the source pins (below), `availability()`, and the probe on the loading thread; a compile or probe failure, pin drift and any exception (logged with its traceback) are `warnings.warn` plus `unavailable`. The probe sets `_serving` only while it runs, leaves both flags clear, restores `calls`, drops the mirrors it built and calls `mx.clear_cache()` in `finally`, before the engine measures the budget.
- The probe, numbered (inputs are GPU-built and evaluated before any launch; the fp32 reference is fp32 SDPA on the GPU over the dequantised K, V and Q codes): (1) quantiser against the reference on random rows and on tie rows, bitwise; bulk equals incremental (one build against rows added in two steps); (2) kernel exactness: relative RMS ≤ 1e-2 against the reference at N0 and the far mark, T = 1, 5, 8, including ×8 queries with a dominant key at n − 1 and a decoy at n; (3) parity: every verify row `array_equal` its one-row call at N0, the next two step boundaries and the far mark, T = 1..8 at the tile's three placements; (4) fence: NaN scales and −128 codes past n, and buffers ending exactly at n, bitwise equal to the clean run and finite; (5) the mirror rule on a real `KVCache`: build, trim, append without a served call, append with one; the shrinking-block rejection across N0 (16 rows served, trim below N0, 8 rows below N0 unserved, then a served call) ends with the mirror equal to a bulk re-quantisation; a stale serial rebuilds; a capacity not a multiple of 4 is handled; (6) quantisation error against stock bf16 SDPA on Gaussian K/V ≤ 3e-2 (the format's own error there is about 0.7 %); (7) the model's own attention module through the decode hook and the verifier's wrapper with `_serving` set, `calls` showing `decode_kv8` and `verify_kv8`, each within 3e-2 of the same module on the bf16 path.
- Source pins `VALIDATED_KV8_SOURCES` (sha256 of `inspect.getsource`, following `__wrapped__`): `KVCache.trim`, `KVCache.prefix_cache_reserve`, `cache_state.start_speculative_cache`, `SpeculativeCacheTransaction.commit`, `.abort`, `_trim_append_caches`, `commit_speculative_round`, `rollback_speculative_cache`, `LanguageModel.rollback_speculative_cache` and `Qwen3_5BatchInvariantForward.verify`: the mirror's validity rule assumes verify rows are appended through `update_and_fetch` and rolled back through `trim`, never replayed; a path that replayed accepted rows (mlx-vlm's rotating-cache path already does) would go stale with every gate green. Checked in `enable()` (a warn refusal) and by a CI hash test like verifyattn's.
- `clear()` from `VLMEngine.unload()`: both flags, and nothing else (mirrors live on caches that are dropped with the engine).

### Engine, config, status, tune

- `config.py`: `kv_int8: bool = True` on `SousConfig`, parsed by `_kv_int8` with the warn-and-False rule; `_KNOWN["model"]` gains the key.
- `base.default_engine_factory` passes `kv_int8=config.kv_int8`; `VLMEngine.__init__` takes `kv_int8: bool = False`, enables it after `projkernel.enable` (so the tile's state is settled) and before the fork store and `measure_cache_budget` (so the probe's arrays are released first). `reserve_bytes` and `kv_bytes_per_token` are untouched (decision 6).
- `VLMEngine.decode()`: sets `_serving` (and bumps the serial) around the `stream_generate` loop and in `finally` clears it and drops every layer's mirror; `unload()` calls `kv8.clear()`. `LMEngine.kv_int8_status = {"state": "off", "reason": None, "probe_seconds": None}`; `ManagedEngine.kv_int8_status` passes through by `getattr`.
- The load line carries `kv_int8=<state>` and, when active, `kv_int8_probe_s=`; the status document's `engine` block carries `kv_int8: {state, reason}`; `docs/observability.md` lists both.
- `sous tune`: `Arm.kv_int8` (mirrored from the user config in `arms.py` like `attention_tile`), in `Arm.suite_key`; `SuiteRun.kv_int8` and `SuiteRun.key`, with `from_dict` defaulting to `False` for rows written before the key existed; the runner's `replace(kv_int8=arm.kv_int8)` and a `_check_kv8` notice from `engine.kv_int8_status` that names `unavailable`, never `off`; `docs/tuning.md` gets the paragraph the tile has. `candidates.fit()` is unchanged: the mirror is admitted against live headroom, never sized into the window.
- `forkstore.engine_epoch`: excludes `attention_tile_kv8.metal` and `kv8_quant.metal` by name, with the reason from decision 7; a test pins the exclusion.

## Merge gate (pre-registered 2026-10-12, before any in-model number)

Preconditions: main at or after the relay merge (`feat/attention-tile-split-qk`); the branch tree in its own worktree and venv on the M5 Pro; every job under `gpu_run_m5.py` with the production daemon idle. Verdict: **ship** only if steps 1–6 all pass; a failure in a step with a close or keep-off rule closes as that step says; anything else goes to the maintainer.

## Departures from the spike's registered stage-2 shape (recorded here, with reasons)

- The mirror is built lazily inside `decode()` and follows trims, not written on every append: prefill never pays, forks never carry it, and the per-round gate shim becomes possible (the spike said the format could not be drawn per round).
- No fork-key field and no `_EPOCH_FILES` entry: nothing served is persisted.
- No reserve scaling: admission against live headroom instead, for the slot-budget reason in decision 6.
- Per-round arms are the primary speed gate and per-daemon ABBA the secondary, where the spike listed them the other way.
- Stage 1's rule (d) was missed by the mirror path (0.46 ms against 0.30) and the maintainer ruled past it; the gate's step 2 carries the cost as a dependent-chain cell instead.

## Out of scope

- An int8-only cache (the memory win: smaller slots, forks and reserve). It needs a cache class forkio, fork_copy, every `type(cache) is KVCache` gate and the fork layout version admit; the mirror keeps those untouched and can become that later.
- Group-64 K scales (the quality fallback): needs the group-aligned head-dim permutation; built only if step 6 fails at per-token.
- Prefill attention over int8 history.
- Changing the default block: the relay PR's #157 retest decides that.
